In an era of automation and layoffs, be known for the opposite.

Calling all human first companies

calling_all_companies.txt_□×

Every year, the headlines about technology are the same: layoffs, automation, and another job that AI can do a little cheaper. Inside that story, the companies getting the human side right rarely get mentioned at all.

For five days, we brought together organizations of every kind — tech, healthcare, finance, education, and beyond — who are leading with people-first principles.

Hosts joined one of our flagship conversations or ran their own program. The only requirement was a shared desire to see humans flourish in an age where authenticity is increasingly hard to come by.

Who can host?

Anyone. Mostly.

Our mission at Human Week is to fill a week with conversations that are worth your time to have: conversations that change your perspective, introduce you to someone new, and lead to inspiring, pro-human collaborations. So long as this calls to you, you are part of Human Week.

We ask everyone who hosts during Human Week to start with a real question, take a clear stance, and put human needs first. That's it. What followed was a week of rooms where people said what they think, heard something they hadn't considered, and left with new relationships, ideas and excitement.

Anyone with a stake in the future is welcome to host a Human Week conversation. As far as we can tell, that's everybody.

Five house rules:

  1. Name the topic and why it matters
  2. Cut the industry jargon
  3. Design for participation
  4. Treat comfort as an access issue
  5. Provide a thesis (aka "your why")

Gathering types we support

Part of the Human Week philosophy is that quality events lead to quality outcomes. We support gathering models that include all and encourage productive connection and conversation.

gathering_types.cfg_□×

Point of View Panels

This is a specific, structured, and moderated conversation designed to elicit disagreement productively. It brings together two or three voices who disagree or approach the same question from genuinely different lived angles, and is moderated towards friction rather than consensus. We suggest about twenty minutes of discussion and debrief afterwards, with framing questions that ask people to reflect positively on what they learned or took away from the session.

Working Sessions

Hands-on, ending in something tangible rather than a memory of a talk. Ideal structure involves no more than 10 minutes of set-up and then hands are moving within the first 10 minutes. This includes art classes, yoga, hack-a-thons, and more. Invent your own!

Group Discussions

Salon-style, no stage. Hosted round tables of 5-8, each with a facilitator. The facilitator does not need to be an expert, this is just someone keeping the conversation moving. One shared prompt. Ideal structure takes the “report out” approach, where you start as pairs, then move to groups of four, then eight, then share out to the room.

Communal Meals or Walks

A shared table or shared movement event. Could be a small group dinner, potluck, or a walking conversation instead of a seated program. Walks and meals should be capped at a size where conversation is still possible.

Performances & Talks

Musical acts, single-speaker talks, poetry events, and more fall under this category. Anything that involves light pointed at a human on stage. Ideal structure involves audience interaction with the work through reflection or direct, auditory engagement.
